The Authenticity Problem

Generative music systems are producing content at unprecedented scale. Existing detection systems operate as reactive classifiers that must continuously adapt to newly released generators.

Content Flooding

Millions of AI-generated tracks are entering digital ecosystems, making provenance increasingly difficult to establish.

Detection Arms Race

Most detectors require examples from every new generator, creating an endless retraining cycle.

Trust Erosion

Platforms, labels and rights holders need reliable methods to distinguish authentic creative work from synthetic content.

Human-Centric Detection

Rather than asking whether a song resembles a known AI generator, Auralis asks a more fundamental question:

Does this recording behave like human-created music?

Our system learns the statistical structure of human music exclusively from human recordings. It separates musical content from performance style and models how these components naturally interact.

When those relationships fall outside expected human distributions, the system identifies the recording as potentially synthetic.

Research Foundation

Auralis originated from advanced research in anomaly detection, representation learning, disentanglement and probabilistic modeling.

What makes the technology different?

Unlike conventional detectors, the system never requires AI-generated music during training. The architecture learns only from human recordings and models the conditional relationship between musical content and performance style.

This enables a form of structural generalization that does not depend on collecting examples from every future generator released to the market.
